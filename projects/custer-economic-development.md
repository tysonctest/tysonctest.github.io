Summary

This project analyzed tourism’s role in the economy of Custer County, South Dakota for the Custer Area Economic Development Foundation. The project combined public economic, labor, taxable-sales, lodging, and tourism-related data to help the client better understand how visitor activity shapes local business activity, tax revenue, and regional economic-development planning.

Problem / Research Question

How important is tourism to Custer County’s economy, and what patterns in employment, taxable sales, lodging capacity, and major tourism assets should inform future regional economic-development planning?

Data and Methods

The project used publicly available data from sources such as state government, tourism reports, tax data, labor-market data, and public economic datasets. The analysis focused on identifying practical patterns rather than building a purely academic model.

Methods included:

Public data collection and organization
Tourism employment and labor-income analysis
Taxable-sales trend analysis
Seasonality analysis
Custer City and Custer County comparison
Custer State Park indicator review
Peer-county benchmarking
Client-facing report writing and presentation
Key Findings

The project found that tourism plays an outsized role in Custer County’s local economy. Tourism-related activity supported a large share of employment, labor income, and tax revenue. The analysis also found that local business activity is highly seasonal and strongly connected to visitor activity.

Custer City emerged as the county’s primary commercial hub, generating the large majority of county taxable sales. Custer State Park also appeared to be a central tourism asset, contributing substantially to visitor activity and the surrounding local economy.

Why This Project Matters

This project shows how public data can be translated into practical insights into regional economic development. It also demonstrates the ability to communicate economic findings to a nontechnical client audience and connect data analysis to planning, business activity, and public-finance implications.
